# The 2026 U.S. Midterms and Global Markets

**Impact analysis for U.S. equities, crypto, energy and precious metals, plus a two-year outlook**
*As of 5 November 2026, two days after the election. Prices are closes through 4 Nov 2026.*

---

## 1. Executive summary

**The result.** Democrats won control of **both chambers**. Networks project a House majority, with CBS estimating 251–268 seats, and at least **54 Senate seats**. They flipped TX, ME, IA, OH, NC, SC and KS; Alaska and Mississippi were still uncalled on 4 Nov. Hakeem Jeffries is in line to become Speaker. From 3 Jan 2027 until at least Jan 2029, a Republican president faces a Democratic Congress. Since 1950, this is only the fourth time the president's party lost **both chambers in one night**; the others were 1954, 1994 and 2006.

**The first reaction was a relief rally.** It was not a regime break.

**Our core conclusions for the next 24 months:**

1. **Gridlock, but not a quiet kind.** Congress can now block legislation. It cannot reverse what the White House controls on its own: tariffs (Sections 301/232), the Iran war, regulatory agencies and enforcement. The policy *mix* changes: no new fiscal stimulus, and more shutdown and debt-limit brinkmanship. Policy *volatility* does not fall. Expect headline-driven drawdowns inside an otherwise supportive cycle.
2. **U.S. equities: constructive base case, with changing leadership.** The S&P 500 has risen in the 6 and 12 months after **all 19 midterms since 1950** (mean +14.6% at 12 months). The 30 Sep → 30 Jun window was positive in all 19 cycles (mean +20.1%). The closest analogs (1954, 1994, 2006) returned +30%, +26% and +7%. The risks are valuation (the index is near its high), a Fed with a hiking bias (2Y minus fed funds = **+71bp**), credit already wobbling (HYG/IEF z = −2.0) and **AI-infrastructure regulation** (data-centre moratoria). Prefer broadening, quality and healthcare over concentrated AI-capex exposure.
3. **Crypto: the legislative tailwind is gone, so liquidity and the cycle decide.** The CLARITY Act failed cloture 49–50 on 15 Sep. A Democratic Congress, plus probes of the President's family crypto ventures, makes a market-structure law unlikely before 2029. The macro drivers currently say "not yet": stablecoin supply −4% over 30 days, Fed net liquidity −$80bn over 13 weeks, a firm dollar. The halving cycle enters its historical accumulation phase now; the next halving is around April 2028. Watch for the 2027 debt-limit TGA drawdown, which is historically a liquidity injection.
4. **Energy: the war premium is the swing factor.** Congress does not set it. Brent is still $95 with Hormuz flows around 70% of pre-war levels. Gasoline at $4.15 sits at the 87th percentile of two years, and political pressure to end the war is now bipartisan. Base case: the premium compresses unevenly with sharp spikes. Clean energy gets a policy-sentiment bid, but the OBBBA credit cliff (wind/solar placed in service by end-2027) means 2027 pull-forward and then a 2028 air pocket.
5. **Precious metals: a correction inside a structural bull market.** Real yields of 2.6%, a firm dollar and a Democratic Senate that will vet Fed nominees all remove part of the 2025–26 "politicised Fed / debasement" premium. Central-bank buying (675t YTD), $40tn of debt with ~6% of GDP deficits, a 2027 debt-limit fight and the war are a floor. Gold is below its 200-day average, so trend models are defensive. The gold/silver ratio (78) is neutral. Platinum is weak.

## 2. What the result changes and what it doesn't

### 2.1 Policy-control map (from 3 Jan 2027)

| Policy lever | Who controls it now | Expected direction | Market channel | Most affected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Taxes and spending** | Congress (D) + presidential veto (R) | Gridlock. No new tax cuts or stimulus. Spending fights | Lower fiscal impulse, less Treasury supply growth, lower term premium | Long bonds (+), cyclicals (−), USD (−) |
| **Appropriations** | Congress | Funding expires **11 Dec 2026**. FY2028 deadline 30 Sep 2027 | Shutdown risk. Data blackouts; GDP drag ~0.1–0.2pp per week | Volatility, T-bills |
| **Debt limit** | Congress | Must be addressed by late summer/fall 2027 (Deutsche Bank) unless the lame-duck GOP reconciliation lifts it | TGA run-down before the X-date (liquidity injection), then rebuild (drain). Bill-curve kink | BTC, gold, front-end bills, equities |
| **Tariffs** | **Executive** (Sec. 301/232/122; IEEPA tariffs struck down 20 Feb 2026) | Higher. Goldman expects new hikes; effective rate +~2pp (from 7.1% in Aug, Wharton) | Goods inflation, margins, retaliation, USD | Industrials, retail, autos, EM, PGMs |
| **Iran war** | **Executive**. Congress has war-powers votes and supplemental funding | Pressure to wind down. Risk that a "freed" president escalates (UBS) | Oil risk premium, inflation expectations, Fed path | Oil, energy equities, airlines, gold |
| **Fed** | Chair Warsh (since 2026). **Senate confirms governors** | Senate now Democratic, so a lower risk of Fed capture. FOMC 3.75–4.00% after a September hike | Lower term premium. Less "debasement" demand for gold | Gold (−), long bonds (+), USD (+) |
| **Agencies (SEC/CFTC/EPA/FERC)** | Executive | Deregulation continues through rule-making. Oversight and subpoenas rise | Headline risk more than rule changes | Crypto, banks, energy |
| **Crypto market structure** | Congress | CLARITY failed 49–50 (15 Sep). Prediction markets gave ~6% odds of a law before 1 Jan 2027 | No statutory SEC/CFTC split. Agency-by-agency regime | Alt-coins (−), COIN (−); BTC least affected |
| **AI / data centres** | States + Congressional oversight | Texas governor-elect pledged a data-centre moratorium (TX ≈ 27% of U.S. data-centre GW). Federal AI oversight push | Capex delays, power-price politics | Semis, neoclouds, IPPs, utilities |
| **Healthcare** | Congress | ACA subsidy support | Managed-care and hospital earnings | Molina/Centene/HCA (+) |
| **Energy policy** | Congress (budget) + executive (permits, leasing) | Clean energy and grid priority. Oversight of gas-for-data-centre deals. OBBBA credits cannot be restored over a veto | Relative sentiment, not cash flows | ICLN/TAN vs XLE |

### 2.2 Transmission logic by asset class

```
                 ┌─ fiscal gridlock ──► lower term premium ──► long bonds↑, small caps↑, gold (−)
Democratic ──────┼─ debt-limit fight ──► TGA run-down/rebuild ──► liquidity pulse ──► BTC, gold, equities
Congress vs.     ├─ Senate vets Fed ──► independence priced ──► USD↑ (Koll), gold debasement premium↓
Republican       ├─ oversight/probes ──► AI, crypto, energy-deal headline risk ──► semis↓, COIN↓
White House      └─ no veto override ──► tariffs + war stay executive ──► oil premium, goods inflation, Fed hawkish
```

The key insight: **the variables Congress gained control of are mostly disinflationary and lower volatility** (fiscal restraint, Fed-nominee vetting). **The variables it did not gain are mostly inflationary and raise volatility** (tariffs, war, agency actions). Markets will oscillate between those two forces. That is why the strategy set below combines seasonal and trend exposure with explicit volatility, credit and stress filters.

**Presidential cycle (S&P 500 calendar-year price return, 1950–2025):** post-election year +8.4%, **midterm year +4.6%**, **pre-election year +17.2% (89% positive)**, election year +8.1%. 2027 is a pre-election year.

### 3.2 The closest analogs: the president's party lost both chambers in one night

| Year | Context | S&P +3m | S&P +12m | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1954 | Eisenhower. Post-Korea | +12.9% | **+30.3%** | Gridlock plus disinflation |
| 1994 | Clinton. Fed hiking cycle | +3.3% | **+25.9%** | Fed soft landing in 1995; strong analog for a hawkish Fed |
| 2006 | Bush. **Unpopular war (Iraq)**, Fed at peak rates | +4.0% | **+6.6%** | Oil +62% and gold +34% over the next 12m; then the credit cycle turned in 2007–08 |
| **2026** | Trump. **Unpopular war (Iran)**, Fed just hiked, AI-capex boom | — | — | Valuations far higher than in any analog |

**Honest caveats.** (i) 19 observations with overlapping macro regimes: the 100% hit rate is *partly* a pre-election-year and Fed-reaction effect, not proof of causation. (ii) Commodity and crypto samples are tiny (3–6). (iii) 2006 is the analog that should worry investors most: a war-driven oil shock plus a late-cycle Fed. That combination delivered a positive first year and then a crisis.

### 3.3 What is different in 2026

- **Starting valuation and concentration.** The S&P is within 1.5% of a record, and AI infrastructure is the earnings engine. The AI basket was already −24% from its peak by October (Goldman).
- **The Fed is tightening into the cycle.** It hiked in September 2026, and two FOMC members dissented in favour of another hike in October. The market prices more (2Y − FF = +71bp). Historically, midterm rallies came with a neutral or easing Fed (1994 is the exception that worked).
- **Oil is a war variable, not a cycle variable.** The 2026 Brent path ran from $72 (Feb) to $118 (Mar), $70 (Jul), $110 (Oct) and $95 (now). That volatility comes from geopolitics, so election-cycle oil statistics carry little weight.
- **Gold and silver just went through a blow-off and crash** (Jan 2026: gold ~$5.3–5.6k, silver ~$115). Mean reversion and positioning dominate seasonal tendencies.

## 4. Asset-class deep dives

### 4.1 U.S. equities

**Starting point.** S&P 7,701, 5% above its 200-day average, VIX 17 with a normal term structure (VIX/VIX3M 0.89). Breadth is improving (RSP beat SPY by 2.2% over 63 days), but small caps lag (−4.8%). Semis are de-rating (SMH −12% vs SPY over 63 days). Healthcare leads (+15% vs SPY) and defence lags (−15%).

**Channels.**
- *Positive:* event-risk removal; the post-midterm and pre-election seasonal; fiscal restraint lowering term premium (supports multiples); Fed-independence repricing; a healthcare policy bid; and history (19/19).
- *Negative:* tariff escalation (Goldman: new hikes coming, +~2pp effective rate) squeezes margins in retail, autos and industrials; **AI/data-centre regulation** at state level (Texas, Georgia ≈ ⅓ of AI data-centre projects) and federal oversight; a hawkish Fed; a 11 Dec shutdown and a 2027 debt-limit standoff; investigations and possible impeachment proceedings; and defence budget pressure.

**Sector map for gridlock (12–24m).**

| Overweight bias | Neutral | Underweight bias |
|---|---|---|
| Healthcare/managed care (ACA), staples (affordability politics), equal-weight/mid caps (broadening), regional banks (if the curve steepens; deregulation by agency continues) | Financials, industrials (tariff relief vs tariff cost), utilities (power demand vs data-centre politics) | AI infrastructure/neoclouds (moratoria, power politics), defence (budget fights), private prisons, tariff-exposed importers |

**Scenarios (2-year horizon; subjective probabilities).**

| Scenario | Probability | Description | S&P path |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Productive gridlock + war wind-down | 40% | Short or no shutdown. Debt limit resolved. Iran de-escalation by H1-27. Inflation cools. Fed on hold, then cuts late 2027 | +10–15% over 12m, broadening rally; 2027 a classic pre-election year |
| B. Confrontation | 35% | Tariff escalation, Iran/Hormuz flare-ups, a shutdown, a debt-limit X-date drama, impeachment politics. Fed hikes once more. 10Y > 5.25% | Choppy. One or two 10–15% drawdowns. Flat to +5% |
| C. AI-capex/credit slowdown | 25% | Data-centre moratoria and financing stress hit AI capex. Credit spreads widen. Labour market softens (ADP +86k). Fed pivots to cuts | −20% bear market, then recovery into 2028 |

### 4.2 Crypto

**Starting point.** BTC $80.4k: Mayer multiple 1.10 (just above its 200-day), 30-day vol 32%, 24% below its 1-year high and ~36% below the Oct-2025 ATH. ETH/BTC flat (bitcoin dominance). Stablecoin supply **$295bn, −4.0% over 30 days** (outflow). COIN has lagged BTC by 20% over 63 days, a market-implied *U.S.-specific regulatory discount*.

**Channels.**
- *Legislation:* the CLARITY Act is effectively dead for this Congress. The SEC/CFTC jurisdiction split stays agency-made and reversible after 2028. This matters most for tokens with securities-like features, DeFi and U.S. exchanges, and least for BTC.
- *Oversight:* House and Senate committees will investigate the Trump family's crypto ventures (World Liberty, memecoins). That is a headline risk, not a regulatory change.
- *Stablecoins:* the GENIUS Act (2025) is already law, so issuance growth continues regardless. Stablecoins are also a growing T-bill buyer, which ties crypto liquidity to Treasury financing.
- *Liquidity:* Fed net liquidity (balance sheet − TGA − RRP) is the main macro driver. With the TGA at $1.0tn, the **2027 debt-limit episode** is the single most important crypto-macro event of the window: TGA run-down before the X-date (bullish), rebuild after (bearish). The 2023 analog: BTC rallied into June, then stalled during the rebuild.
- *Cycle:* past halving cycles put tops ~1.5 years after each halving (Oct 2025 fits) and bottoms ~1 year after the top. The model's accumulation phase starts now, with the halving due around April 2028.

**History says:** after the three midterms with BTC data, returns over the next 3 months were negative every time. After 12 months they were +32% on average, with 2018 and 2022 strongly positive.

**Base case.** Range trading with a downside tail while stablecoin and net liquidity contract. The structural re-acceleration becomes more likely from mid-2027 as the debt-limit TGA drawdown and pre-halving positioning line up.

### 4.3 Energy

**Starting point.** Brent $95 / WTI $88, after peaks of $118 (Mar) and $110 (Oct). The Brent–WTI spread is $6.7, a seaborne war premium. The curve is backwardated (USO beat USL by 2.7% over 63 days). Gasoline is $4.15 (87th percentile). ICLN lags XLE by 18% over 63 days despite the election-day bounce.

**Channels.**
- *War premium:* this is the dominant variable. GOP leaders blaming the war for their losses raises the odds of a deal. A president "freed from the pursuit of popularity" (UBS) raises the odds of escalation. Expect **jump risk in both directions**, which favours convex and trend-following exposure over carry.
- *Affordability politics:* gasoline above $4 into a 2028 presidential cycle means SPR policy, export-curb rhetoric and windfall-tax proposals. Without 60 Senate votes these mainly hit producer *equities* (sentiment), not crude.
- *Fed and inflation:* oil passes through to breakevens (2.30% now). A renewed spike would lock in Fed hawkishness and hurt equities.
- *Clean energy:* Democratic control helps sentiment and appropriations. OBBBA's credit phase-out means projects not begun by 4 Jul 2026 must be **placed in service by 31 Dec 2027**, creating a 2027 build rush and a 2028 cliff. Clean energy is also long-duration and suffers when yields rise.
- *Power demand vs politics:* data-centre gas demand faces Democratic scrutiny, a modest negative for natural-gas growth narratives.

**History says:** oil has no reliable post-midterm pattern (3 up, 3 down). The 2006 analog (war plus a lost Congress) saw +62% over 12 months. Treat oil as a geopolitics trade.

### 4.4 Precious metals

**Starting point.** Gold $4,071: −23% from its ATH, ~10% below its 200-day average (downtrend), Q3 average $4,262. Real 10Y yield 2.62% and rising (headwind). Broad USD above its 200-day average. Gold/silver ratio 77.9 (2-year z ≈ 0, neutral). Gold/oil ratio 46, 14% below its 200-day average (miner margins squeezed, now improving as oil falls). Platinum/gold at a 1-year z of −1.4 (PGMs weak).

**Channels.**
- *Lower Fed-capture risk:* the Senate gatekeeps Fed nominees. Part of the 2025–26 premium came from fear of a politicised Fed, and it fades (bearish at the margin).
- *Fiscal:* gridlock caps new deficits (mildly bearish). Debt-limit brinkmanship and $40tn of debt keep a sovereign-risk bid (bullish in episodes).
- *Geopolitics:* the war and tariffs are stress hedges. Central-bank buying (Q3 321t, 675t YTD; WGC forecasts ~850t for 2026) is the structural floor.
- *Real rates:* 2.6% real yields are restrictive. Any Scenario-C pivot to cuts is the most bullish gold path.
- *Silver/PGMs:* industrial beta to tariffs (Section 232 critical-minerals probes), autos and solar. Silver fell 55% from its peak; as a high-beta asset it needs a gold uptrend first.

**History says:** gold rose in the 6 months after all six midterms since 2002 (mean +18.6% at 12 months). 2006 (+34%) is again the war analog.
