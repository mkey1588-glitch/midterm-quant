The 5 November selloff

The S&P 500 fell 4.4% on Thursday 5 November, its worst day since April 2025, as the AI trade unwound. Chip stocks had their worst day since March 2020 and the Nasdaq-100 lost 6.4%. The index closed 0.4% above its 200-day average, so the trend rules keep their stock exposure. The volatility rule cuts, and three crypto rules head to cash on Monday. The average stock, the dollar and junk bonds held up, which so far marks this as a sector shock rather than a market-wide one.

Reading the close

The rules respond in proportion. Only one of the 33 indicators changed state, the VIX curve, so the cuts come from the volatility and crypto rules. The trend rules hold because the S&P held its 200-day line. Friday is the test. A further fall of about 0.7% would break the line the rules use, flipping E1 at Monday's rebalance and blocking E5. A flat or slightly lower close would trigger E5's dip buy instead.

What changed on 3 November

Gridlock arrives, but policy volatility stays. Congress can now block taxes, spending and Fed nominees. It cannot undo what the White House controls alone.

Congress now controls mostly lowers inflation and volatility

  • Taxes and spendingNo new tax cuts or stimulus. Lower fiscal impulse and Treasury supply growth.
  • AppropriationsFunding expires 11 Dec 2026, then 30 Sep 2027. Each is a shutdown window.
  • Debt limitMust be raised by late summer or fall 2027 unless the lame-duck session lifts it.
  • Fed confirmationsA Democratic Senate vets nominees, so the risk of a politicised Fed falls.
  • Crypto market structureCLARITY failed 49–50 in September. No law likely before 2029.

The White House keeps mostly raises inflation and volatility

  • TariffsSections 301, 232 and 122. Goldman expects new hikes, about +2pp on the effective rate.
  • The Iran warCongress has only War Powers votes and funding requests. Brent still carries a war premium.
  • AgenciesSEC, CFTC, EPA and FERC keep deregulating by rule-making. Oversight hearings add headline risk.
  • Energy permits and leasingExecutive. Clean-energy tax credits cannot be restored over a veto.
  • AI and data centresSet by states. Texas's governor-elect has pledged a data-centre moratorium.

Markets will swing between these two sets of forces. That is why the strategies below pair seasonal and trend exposure with explicit volatility, credit and stress filters.

Treat these with care. Nineteen overlapping observations cannot prove cause; part of the pattern is the pre-election year and the Fed's reaction. The commodity and crypto samples have three to six cases. 2006 is the analog to worry about most: a war-driven oil shock and a late-cycle Fed gave a positive first year, then a crisis.

How to read the results

  • The equity and metals strategies mostly earn less than buy-and-hold in exchange for much smaller drawdowns.
  • The crypto strategies beat bitcoin on a risk-adjusted basis, and since 2018 in absolute terms too.
  • No oil-timing rule tested had a standalone edge from 2009 to 2026, a period with two price collapses and USO's 2020 restructuring.
  • The five energy strategies barely move together (average correlation −0.01), so their blend has a Sharpe of 0.59 and a −10% worst drop. Use them only as a diversified sleeve.

Known limitations

  • Small samples for event effects: 19 midterms, three halving cycles.
  • ETF proxies (USO, UGA, USL) carry roll costs. There is no futures-curve data.
  • The thematic ETFs (TAN, SMH, ICLN) were chosen with 2026 hindsight.
  • Yahoo prices and DefiLlama history can be revised; FRED publication lags are approximations.
  • No capacity, borrow availability or tax modelling. Crypto is spot only.
  • 2026 combines a hawkish Fed, a war-driven oil price and AI concentration, with no clean precedent.

How the code is built

  • Each strategy implements one method that returns target weights, such as SPY 98%. It can only read history up to the current bar.
  • Macro series (FRED, DefiLlama) enter as signal-only feeds, shifted to their publication dates.
  • Orders fill at the next open, sells first. Buys that bounce on an overnight gap are retried the next bar.
  • Per-asset costs, fractional sizes for crypto, a 1% borrow fee on shorts and T-bill interest on idle cash.
  • 57 offline tests, including one that runs every strategy on full and truncated data and checks that past decisions do not change.

Moving to a live framework

Because every strategy outputs a weight dictionary, porting is mechanical. In LEAN, the weights become set_holdings calls on a schedule and USO/GLD become continuous futures. In NautilusTrader, they become market orders sized from weight changes. The indicator library has no Backtrader dependency and ports unchanged.

Risks of the choice: Backtrader is unmaintained (last release 2023) and GPL-licensed. Versions are pinned and the engine code sits in one folder, so the test suite flags breakage at once.
